Holly Gibney Reading Order: Every Book and Story She Appears In

2026-11-05

Holly Gibney started out as a supporting character — an anxious, socially awkward woman who wasn't even supposed to be in Mr. Mercedes for very long. Stephen King has said more than once that she simply wouldn't leave; he kept writing her because he couldn't stop being interested in her. Nine years and seven books later, she's arguably the most durable character he's created outside of Roland Deschain, and she's carried an entire corner of his bibliography on her own.

That makes her reading order a little more tangled than most King series, because she starts as a side character in a crime trilogy, grows into the lead of a supernatural series, and eventually becomes the first King character to anchor two solo novels back to back. Here's the order that actually works, and what each book does with her.

The full Holly Gibney reading order

  1. Mr. Mercedes (2014)
  2. Finders Keepers (2015)
  3. End of Watch (2016)
  4. The Outsider (2018)
  5. If It Bleeds (2020), the title novella in the collection of the same name
  6. Holly (2023)
  7. Never Flinch (2025)

Publication order is the right order here, full stop. Unlike some of King's looser Castle Rock connections, Holly's books track her personal growth in a specific sequence — from the trembling, tic-ridden woman who can barely make eye contact in Mr. Mercedes to the confident, licensed private investigator running her own agency in Never Flinch. Reading out of order doesn't just risk spoilers, it scrambles the actual point of the series, which is watching her change.

Where she starts: the Bill Hodges trilogy

Holly's first three appearances are technically Bill Hodges' story, not hers. In Mr. Mercedes, she's introduced as a relative of one of the victims of a mass-casualty attack, pulled into retired detective Hodges' unofficial investigation almost by accident. She's medicated, agoraphobic, and treated by her own family as a lost cause — and then she turns out to be the one who stops the killer, Brady Hartsfield, from detonating a bomb at a packed concert.

Finders Keepers sidelines her somewhat in favor of a separate plot about a stolen literary manuscript, though she and Hodges have by then formalized their partnership into a real detective agency. End of Watch brings her fully back to the center, pairing her against a psychically empowered Brady in the trilogy's darkest, most supernatural turn. By the end of that book, Holly isn't a bystander anymore — she's a working investigator who's faced something genuinely monstrous and held her own. For the full breakdown of that trilogy on its own terms, see our Bill Hodges trilogy reading order guide.

You can technically skip straight to The Outsider without reading the Hodges books first — King writes it to stand alone — but you'd be starting with a version of Holly who's already fully formed, and you'd miss the arc that makes her later confidence actually mean something.

The solo run: The Outsider through Never Flinch

The Outsider is where the series shifts from "Holly as supporting character" to "Holly as the person the story needs." A boy is murdered in a small Oklahoma town, the evidence against a popular, well-liked suspect is airtight and physically impossible at the same time, and the local detectives are stuck — so they call in Holly, now running her own practice, to make sense of a case that doesn't follow normal rules. It's the book where King commits fully to the idea that Holly's specific mind, methodical and literal and unbothered by what "makes sense," is suited to exactly the kind of case regular investigators can't crack.

If It Bleeds is a direct sequel, collected as the lead novella in the four-story book of the same name, and it's built entirely around Holly investigating a suicide bombing that doesn't add up. It's shorter and tighter than The Outsider, essentially a standalone mystery that happens to deepen her character and her understanding of what she survived in the previous book.

Holly (2023) is the big turning point: the first novel where she's the unambiguous lead from page one, no Hodges, no ensemble framing. She investigates the disappearance of a college student and uncovers a pair of elderly, cannibalistic academics — a plot that's as grounded and human-scale monstrous as King gets, no shapeshifters required. It's also notably set during the early pandemic, which King uses to sharpen Holly's isolation and her discomfort asking for help, themes that have run through her character since book one.

Never Flinch (2025) continues straight on from there, with Holly juggling two cases at once — a killer who's announced he intends to murder thirteen innocent people in revenge for a wrongful conviction, and a stalker targeting a women's-rights activist. It's the first time Holly has had a true direct sequel built around her alone rather than around a shared mystery, and it leans further into procedural thriller territory than the more overtly supernatural Outsider and If It Bleeds. You don't need total recall of the earlier books to follow it, but her working relationships with recurring side characters (Pete Huntley, her own office staff) land better if you've been with her since Holly.

Do you need to read the Bill Hodges trilogy first?

Not strictly. The Outsider was written to work as an entry point, and King includes enough backstory that new readers aren't lost. But Holly's whole emotional trajectory — the entire point of her as a character — only really lands if you've seen where she started. Reading the Hodges trilogy first costs you three extra books; skipping it costs you the thing that makes her interesting. If you've got the time, start at the top.

The adaptations

Holly has already been played by two different actors in two unrelated productions, which is part of why keeping the reading order straight matters. Justine Lupe played her in the Mr. Mercedes TV series (2017–2019) for the Audience Network, a lighter, more comic take on the character that aired across three seasons in a shuffled order (it adapted the books as Season 1, 3, 2 rather than straight through). Cynthia Erivo played a very different, more withdrawn version of Holly in HBO's 2020 miniseries adaptation of The Outsider, opposite Ben Mendelsohn and Jason Bateman; HBO passed on a second season despite scripts reportedly being written for one.

A TV adaptation of the Holly novel has reportedly been in development since 2024, with Mr. Mercedes producer Jack Bender attached, though no cast or release date has been confirmed as of this writing. Given how well The Outsider performed for HBO, a further Holly adaptation wouldn't be surprising — but until something is officially announced, treat it as a rumor rather than a release to wait for.

Should you start here?

If you've already read It or The Shining and want to see a very different side of King — patient, character-driven, more interested in grief and resilience than in cosmic horror — the Holly books are one of the best on-ramps in his catalog. Start with Mr. Mercedes if you want her full arc from the beginning, or jump straight to The Outsider if you'd rather start with the supernatural material and work backward later. Either way, read the rest in order once you're hooked; this is one series where the order really does matter.
